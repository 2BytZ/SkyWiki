Dranleic Haligdrake (or Dranleic "Revakdovah" lit. "Holy-Dragon" in Dovahzul), also known as the Dragonborne, the Rider of Night, Dragonborn of Darkness, and Alduin's Scale, is a male Argonian warrior, thief and assassin, said to be the Dragonborn of legend. Having ties to notable criminal factions such as the Thieves Guild and the Dark Brotherhood, he also has connections with people in positions of power in Skyrim, such as the Jarls. Dranleic hails from Cyrodiil in Tamriel. His true age and potential parents are unknown; however, based on physical appearance and attributes, it is believed he was born in the last half-century. Dranleic is a notorious force of evil and mischief in Skyrim. Although it is said that he is the Dragonborn, many citizens disagree because of the various misdeeds he has committed and chaos he has caused during his time in Skyrim.

Dranleic has been largely involved in some of the biggest criminal events Tamriel has experienced since The Assassination of Emperor Uriel Septim VII and has played a major hand in those criminal operations, often being the centerpiece of the operations — operations such as The Attempted Assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II and the subsequent successful assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II, The Escape of Cidhna Mine with Madanach and the bloodbath that followed, The Assassination of Vittoria Vici, the soon-to-be spouse of Asgier Snow-Shod, along with the takeovers of the major holds of Skyrim: the city of Whiterun, Falkreath, Markarth, and Solitude.

Early life

Little is known about Dranleic's origins or early life. His earliest documented appearance was at Helgen, where he was sentenced to execution along with other thieves, Stormcloak rebels, and Ulfric Stormcloak. No records about him before this event have been found. Imperial Legion soldiers and the few survivors from the dragon attack at Helgen described him as "having seemingly appeared from nowhere," and said he was "unfortunately bundled together with other petty thieves and bandits ready for the chopping block."[ref] An account from an anonymous Stormcloak soldier stated Dranleic was trying to cross the border out of Skyrim to return home but was then "caught in an Imperial ambush."[ref]

Due to the absence of direct evidence, multiple theories have arisen. Scholars and bardic tales have suggested that he originated in Cyrodiil: if, as the Stormcloak soldier claimed, he was crossing the border to return home, his home must have lain on the other side. Although Argonians are native to the province of Black Marsh, they live throughout Tamriel, so scholars cannot say with certainty whether he was born in Cyrodiil or elsewhere.

Some also theorize that he is an orphan, citing his apparent lack of contact with any relatives as well as the fact that he has made no known attempts to return home since the events at Helgen.[citation needed] Some researchers have dismissed rumors that the Dragonborn are immortal,[ref] and estimate from his appearance that he was born in the Fourth Era, between 4E 160 and 4E 180. The exact date of his birth is uncertain, though two independent kinds of sources point to the same month. One group claims to have seen Dranleic celebrate his birthday, stating it falls on the 6th of First Seed, while sources from mages' studies suggest he was born sometime during that month, on the grounds that his nature matches that of the Lord birthsign.[ref]

Arrival in Skyrim

Dranleic's documented activity begins in Skyrim in 4E 201, when he was captured near the Cyrodiil border and taken to Helgen for execution. He survived Alduin's attack on Helgen and avoided execution, then made his way to Whiterun, where his part in the death of the dragon Mirmulnir led witnesses to hail him as the Dragonborn. Soon afterward, he was summoned to High Hrothgar, where the Greybeards reportedly confirmed that he is Dragonborn, but this remains disputed by some of Skyrim's citizens.

Escape from Helgen

Main article: Attack on Helgen

On the 17th of Last Seed, 4E 201, Dranleic was captured by Imperial forces near the Pale Pass on the Cyrodiil-Skyrim border.[ref] He was taken to Helgen alongside Stormcloak rebels, other prisoners, and Ulfric Stormcloak, Jarl of Windhelm. Imperial soldiers mistook Dranleic for a Stormcloak and sentenced him to execution with the rest. General Tullius later described the whole ordeal with Dranleic as "a big misunderstanding"[ref]. When Dranleic was called forward, he approached the headsman. Just before his execution, a black dragon, later identified as Alduin, attacked the town. In the chaos, Stormcloak prisoners scattered and fled to the Keep, where the Imperial soldiers' barracks were located. Dranleic was unbound by a Stormcloak, and he fought alongside them through Legionnaires to escape through Helgen Keep.[ref]

Whiterun

After escaping Helgen, Dranleic traveled to Riverwood, where one of the escapees had connections, and after a short rest continued to Whiterun. "I'd never seen an Argonian so stone-faced in my life, but he claimed to have had news from Helgen about the dragon attack," a former Whiterun guard stated, and he was admitted.[ref] At Dragonsreach, he told the then-Jarl Balgruuf the Greater of what he had seen at Helgen, and the jarl believed him. Balgruuf's court wizard, Farengar Secret-Fire, who had been studying dragons, reportedly welcomed the news with enthusiasm and asked Dranleic to recover a Dragonstone from Bleak Falls Barrow, an ancient Nord tomb in the mountains above Riverwood.[ref]

A dead Dunmer

Dranleic cleared the bandits and draugr from the barrow and retrieved the Dragonstone from its inner sanctum, where he also took an ancient nord battle axe from the draugr guarding the stone.[ref] Journeymen, who later investigated the barrow reported finding the body of a Dunmer near a chamber where Dranleic had freed him from a Frostbite spider's web.[ref]

When Dranleic delivered the Dragonstone to Farengar, Irileth, Balgruuf's housecarl, reported that a dragon had been sighted at the watchtower west of Whiterun. A company of guards marched out, and Balgruuf advised Dranleic to join them. They found the tower in ruins, and the dragon, later identified as Mirmulnir, attacked soon after. Irileth, the guards, and Dranleic fought it; several guards were killed, but the dragon was slain.[ref] Eyewitnesses stated that Dranleic "absorbed its soul" as the dragon's body burned away to bone, and onlookers took this as a sign that he was Dragonborn.[ref]

Greybeards' summons

Soon after the dragon's death, a thunderous voice from the mountains was heard calling "DOVAHKIIN!", which was the voice of the Greybeards of High Hrothgar.[ref] Dranleic first reported back to Balgruuf, who named him Thane of Whiterun (See Titles and honors section) and told him, "You'd better get up to High Hrothgar immediately. There's no refusing the summons of the Greybeards."[ref] He then traveled to Ivarstead, where residents recalled seeing him somewhere around the 18th of Last Seed, 4E 201, before he climbed the 7,000 Steps to the monastery.[ref] At High Hrothgar, the Greybeards tested him, taught him the ways of the Voice, or Thu'um, and gave him knowledge of the words FUS and RO (the first two words of Unrelenting Force) and WULD (the first word of Whirlwind Sprint). They found him not only Dragonborn but unusually gifted in the Voice.[ref]

Escape from Cidhna Mine

On the [date] of [month], 4E 201, Dranleic was arrested by the guards of Markarth at the Shrine of Talos for murder and conspiracy against the city of Markarth, as stated by the guards.[ref] He had reportedly been seen snooping around the city, investigating a so-called "Forsworn Conspiracy" following the murder of Margret, who was killed right in the market in broad daylight by a Breton named Weylin, a Forsworn spy. Dranleic was believed to be guilty of murdering Thonar Silver-Blood's wife, Betrid Silver-Blood, along with the Treasury House cleaning crew, Nana Ildene and Donnel after breaking into Thonar's quarters, the residents of Nepos's house, those being the owner, Nepos the Nose, and his servants; Morven, Tynan, and Uaile. However, it was later discovered that they were also Forsworn in disguise and they were to blame for Betrid's death.

Dranleic wearing the Armor of the Old Gods

These actions, reportedly set in motion by Eltrys, a Breton citizen, damaged Dranleic's reputation in Markarth and landed him in Cidhna Mine. There, he escaped with Madanach through a secret tunnel to Markarth Ruins along with the other Forsworn prisoners. One of the prisoners, Grisvar, was also found shanked to death with a makeshift Shiv made of a sharpened stick, at the back of the mine. It is unclear if this too was Dranleic's doing. After escaping through the ruins and emerging on the other side in Markarth, he and the Forsworn, as well as Madanach, confronted Thonar, accompanied by a Markarth City guard. Here, Dranleic was seen wearing the Armor of the Old Gods, given to him by the Forsworn. After a short speech by Thonar, the battle between the city guard and Thonar, and the Forsworn and Dranleic began. Thonar, the guards, and some of the citizens of Markarth were all killed, and Madanach and the rest of the Forsworn army, as well as Dranleic, ran to Druadach Redoubt when they made it outside the city gates.

Thieves Guild

It is widely known that Dranleic is a member of the Thieves Guild. However, due to the nature of the Thieves Guild, the details surrounding how this came to be are very thin. It is known that the Thieves Guild is a secretive organization and one that does not speak openly about its business or its members. All their operations happen underground and in secret.

The first recorded Thieves Guild activity involving Dranleic was on the [day] of [month], 4E 201, when he went door to door in Riften, demanding payment from business owners that they supposedly owed. Dranleic visited the Bee and Barb, the Pawned Prawn, and Haelga's Bunkhouse. The innkeeper, Keerava of the Bee and Barb, was initially reluctant to pay, but after he threatened her, she handed over what she had, and Dranleic left the premises. The owner of the Pawned Prawn, Bersi, stated, "I refused to give up any coin, then [Dranleic] smashed my precious urn! It was priceless, y'know? Priceless! Now it's in pieces. Tiny, little, useless pieces. He threatened to break more of my stuff, so I gave him the damn coin and told him to never come back!" Haelga of Haelga's Bunkhouse stated she initially refused Dranleic, whereupon he left. When Dranleic returned to the Bunkhouse after visiting the other establishments, Haelga paid him without hesitation, fearing what might happen otherwise. Haelga later discovered that her statue of Dibella had been stolen and claimed that Dranleic was responsible.

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, a break-in at the Dwemer Museum in Markarth was reported. The same day, carriage drivers and Markarth guards reported seeing Dranleic travel to and enter Markarth city. No evidence or traces were found at the museum, but local reports tie it to Dranleic and the Thieves Guild, though nothing was reported stolen and no connection has ever been confirmed.

Following this, on the [day] of [month], 4E 201, a break-in at Riftweald Manor was reported in Riften, which was, at the time, owned by Mercer Frey, leader of the Thieves Guild in Skyrim. The manor was found completely empty, and its Nord doorman, Vald, lay dead outside the front entrance. Riften guards stated "There is no question about it; this was definitely the work of the Thieves Guild." To the residents of Riften, this was rather unusual. The Thieves Guild normally does not engage in infighting, especially not with its leader. Riften officials started to investigate the situation, but later dropped it. When residents asked why, they received no comment and were asked to leave.

Dranleic wearing a non-standard Thieves Guild uniform

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, the ruins of Irkngthand collapsed for unknown reasons. Investigators searching the rubble after its collapse found the remains of a man, later identified as Mercer Frey. It is unclear whether this is related to the events of the break-in at Riftweald Manor. Not long after Mercer's death, illegal activity and reports of burglary and thievery rose sharply across Skyrim. Reporters link the surge to the apparent change in leadership at the Thieves Guild following the death of the previous Guildmaster. This sudden spike in Thieves Guild activity sparked renewed fear among citizens. Although it remains unverified who the new leader of the guild is, witnesses have reported seeing Dranleic wearing a black, bluish-tinted uniform unlike the brown attire of ordinary guild thieves, suggesting a higher position in the guild.

Companions

Dranleic had a brief stint with the Companions. He joined them in Whiterun after approaching their leader at the time, Kodlak Whitemane, known as the Harbinger, and asking to join. By this point, Dranleic had made a bit of a name for himself, as word had gotten around that he had slain a dragon and was rumored to be the Dragonborn. To begin with, the members wondered why he would want to join them, as they felt the Companions were “a bit below his pay-grade”. Nonetheless, his request was accepted, and he was taken to the sparring ground at the back of Jorrvaskr to test his mettle in a short duel, which ended quickly.

After this, Dranleic was sent on small jobs, such as settling an argument between two Whiterun residents, and on an errand to fetch a piece of Ysgramor’s Axe alongside Farkas, another member of the Companions. Following these tasks, however, he stopped appearing at Jorrvaskr, and the Companions noted a prolonged absence.

Later, after his eventual return and an attack on the Companions by the Silver Hand, he went with other members to retrieve the pieces of Ysgramor’s Axe that the Silver Hand had stolen. In the course of these events, two important members of the Companions died, one of them Kodlak, and a ceremony was held at the Sky Forge. The Companions say that Kodlak named him the new leader. However, after these events, Dranleic seems to have separated himself from the Companions, and he has not returned since being named leader.

Dark Brotherhood

The Penitus Oculatus date Dranleic's membership in the Dark Brotherhood from the murder of Grelod the Kind in Riften in 4E 201. Over the following months, he is believed to have killed at least sixteen people across Skyrim, most of them identified by a distinctive mark found on the bodies. His public murder of Vittoria Vici in Solitude allowed investigators to link the earlier deaths to him, and his activities culminated in the assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II, which ended up getting the Dark Brotherhood sanctuary destroyed.

Background

During his time in Skyrim, Dranleic is said to have killed at least sixteen people in the name of the Dark Brotherhood. His first known association with the Brotherhood came on the [day] of [month], 4E 201, when he was seen entering and leaving the house of Aventus Aretino, where locals rumored the Black Sacrament had been performed to summon the Dark Brotherhood. The same day, Grelod the Kind was murdered by Dranleic at the Honorhall Orphanage in Riften, in front of the orphans and her assistant, Constance Michel. Members of the Penitus Oculatus believe this marks the beginning of Dranleic's membership in the Dark Brotherhood.

A couple of days later, on the [day] of [month], 4E 201, three people were found dead at an abandoned shack in Hjaalmarch, about ten minutes' walk east of Solitude. They were identified as Fultheim the Fearless, Alea Quintos, and a Khajiit named Vasha. Each had been killed by a single deep cut, which investigators attributed to a sword, running from the upper left shoulder across the torso to the lower right ribs. Whether Dranleic was responsible has never been established, and the wound differs from the distinctive cut later found on his victims.

Dranleic is believed to have killed many more across Skyrim, among them Narfi, a beggar in Ivarstead; Beitild, the owner of Iron-Breaker Mine in Dawnstar; and Ennodius Papius, a former lumberjack at Anga's Mill, west of Windhelm. The Penitus Oculatus attribute these and other deaths to Dranleic because each victim bore the same deep cut down their backs as Vittoria Vici, whom witnesses saw him kill. The priests and priestesses of Arkay who tend the Halls of the Dead across the holds of Skyrim worked together to match the wounds. Murder of Vittoria Vici

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, Vittoria Vici and her fiancé, Asgeir Snow-Shod, were set to be married in Solitude at the Temple of the Divines. The ceremony was scheduled for [time] and Vittoria was to give a speech to the assembled guests. The wedding festival was an open event, so anyone could walk in and out as they pleased.

Dranleic murdering the bride at the Temple of the Divines

Eyewitnesses reported that Dranleic arrived at [time] and spoke with some of the guests before taking a seat. Spectators stated, "When Vittoria got up to give a speech and welcome the guests, [Dranleic] got up and left." At this point, he slipped into the entryway and up to the balcony where Vittoria and Asgeir were standing. At [time], while she was giving her speech, Vittoria Vici was murdered in cold blood by Dranleic Haligdrake in front of the assembled guests, the Solitude guards, and Asgeir Snow-Shod. Dranleic was chased by the city guard, and made his way towards the stairwell under the Castle Dour tower bridge, near the market, which leads out of the city to the docks and escaped.

Following her death, the temple scene was investigated, and was searched for traces of the murderer. When guards inspected Vittoria Vici's body, they noticed that the wound bore a striking resemblance to those found on other victims reported across Skyrim before the wedding. One deep cut running down the victim's back. This was immediately reported to the Penitus Oculatus and other relevant authorities for further investigation.

Murder of Gaius Maro

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, Gaius Maro, the son of Commander Maro of the Penitus Oculatus, was found dead on his way to Solitude from Dragon Bridge. A letter implicating him in a plot to kill the emperor was found on his person. No wound resembling the mark on earlier victims was found on him, but Commander Maro claimed this was the work of the Dark Brotherhood in a ploy to disrupt the Penitus Oculatus's efforts to protect the emperor. It has not been confirmed who is responsible for Gaius's death, but previous records suggest Dranleic is the prime suspect.

Murder of the Mysterious Gourmet

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, the corpse of an Orc was found at Nightgate Inn in an empty mead barrel in the inn's cellar. The innkeeper found the corpse after his "important guest" went missing and claimed the body to be that of the legendary Gourmet, the author behind the cookbook Uncommon Taste, who had been a special guest at the inn for an extended period of time. The Orc bore the same mark seen on other victims such as Vittoria Vici, implying that this was also the work of the Dark Brotherhood and by extension Dranleic Haligdrake. A priest of Arkay said that the body had been dead for some time before it was found.

Because of how the Gourmet was secretive about his identity, no one was able to identify the Orc by his real name, and the innkeeper's claim about him being the Gourmet is the only reference available to identify the body.

Dranleic murdering the head chef of Understone Keep

The same day, the head chef of Understone Keep in Markarth, Anton Virane, was also killed and he too bore the mark associated with Dranleic. It is unclear whether or how this was related to the death of the Gourmet. A sous chef reported overhearing a conversation with Anton about the whereabouts of the Gourmet and his true identity. However, they stated they were so shaken by seeing Anton be murdered that they could not remember the details of the conversation.

Assassination of Titus Mede II

See also: The Assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II

Dranleic is held responsible for the attempted assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II at Castle Dour, and for the successful assassination that followed aboard the ship Katariah, where sixteen people were found dead, including the emperor and Commander Maro. Because no one present survived, the case against Dranleic rests largely on a note found on the body of Amaund Motierre.

Attempt

Main article: The Attempted Assassination of Emperor Titus Mede II
Dranleic "larping" as the Gourmet

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, the emperor of Tamriel, Titus Mede II, was to dine at Castle Dour on a meal prepared by the legendary Gourmet. However, instead of the real Gourmet, Dranleic arrived in his place, disguised as him, and made his way to the kitchen, where he instructed the chef on how to prepare the potage. Before the potage was finished, he added a "secret ingredient" to the dish: Jarrin Root, which is highly poisonous.

When the dish was ready, Dranleic and the chef brought it to the room where the emperor was waiting, and watched as he tasted it. He praised the dish before choking and dying of the poison. Dranleic fled the castle toward the bridge he had used to escape after the death of Vittoria Vici, where Commander Maro and several other agents confronted him, but he got away. After the events at Castle Dour, the Dark Brotherhood's sanctuary, where its members resided, was found destroyed, and the bodies of its members, including its leader, were discovered. According to Commander Maro, this was their plan: destroying the sanctuary along with killing its members.

According to the Penitus Oculatus, the man at the castle that day was a decoy. Commander Maro stated that he had made a deal with a member of the Dark Brotherhood, under which the Penitus Oculatus would let the Brotherhood continue its business in exchange for Dranleic's life.[ref] However, he said that he had since gone back on his word and would destroy the entire Brotherhood because they killed his son.[ref]

Assassination

The body of Titus Mede II on the Katariah

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201 at dawn, it was discovered by citizens, guards, and fishermen alike that Commander Maro had been murdered on the docks, as well as all of the Penitus Oculatus agents who were stationed on the ship Katariah, Captain Avidus and Lieutenant Salvarus and other sailors. In total, sixteen people, including the emperor, were found dead. The ship was found with dead bodies strewn around through the gangways and galley. Inside the Emperor's Quarters, the emperor, Titus Mede II, lay dead on the floor at the window looking out to the water. People investigating noted with interest that none of the bodies bore the mark associated with Dranleic. The emperor in particular had just one, clean stab wound in his chest, denoting a more "considerate" death. Guardsmen could not directly link the deaths to Dranleic, but given his previous crimes, and since the rest of the Dark Brotherhood in Skyrim was believed to have died when its sanctuary collapsed, the killings were assumed to be his work.

The body of Amaund Motierre at the Bannered Mare

That same morning, at the Bannered Mare in Whiterun city, inside a private room was the body of Amaund Motierre. The body was discovered and reported by the innkeeper, Hulda. The body was linked to Dranleic because it bore the mark seen on his other victims. On the body was a note: "While there are many who would see me dead, there is one who set the machine in motion. This person, whomever he or she may be, must be punished for their treachery. Once you have been rewarded for my assassination, I want you to kill the very person who ordered it. Would you do me this kindness? — Emperor Titus Mede II's last order." This note has been taken as evidence that Dranleic killed the emperor, since no one else present at the Katariah that night survived to hear his last words.

Civil war

See also: Stormcloak Rebellion

During the Skyrim civil war, Dranleic fought for the Stormcloaks, though he has never said why he chose to join them. He took part in the takeover of Whiterun, the capture of Fort Neugrad, Fort Sungard, Fort Snowhawk, and Fort Hraggstad, and the final attack on Solitude, where he killed General Tullius. With most of the Imperials driven out, the war is more or less over, though Ulfric has yet to become High King of Skyrim.

Alliance with the Stormcloaks

Dranleic has not stated why he chose to join the Stormcloaks, but some people theorize it was in part due to what occurred at Helgen. He went to Windhelm to speak with Jarl Ulfric about joining. There, Galmar Stone-Fist instructed him to travel to Serpentstone Isle and defeat an ice wraith that resides there. If Dranleic succeeded, he would be seen as worthy to join the Stormcloaks' ranks. Galmar was surprised by how swiftly Dranleic returned with ice wraith teeth as proof of his accomplishment, not even a day after he had left Windhelm. Ulfric congratulated him and gave a formal speech stating that he was now a part of the Stormcloaks.

Dranleic fighting the draugr with the Jagged Crown

After this, Dranleic's next mission was to join other Stormcloaks on an expedition to acquire the Jagged Crown, which Galmar claimed was located in Korvanjund. There, he and the other Stormcloak soldiers made their way through traps and draugr to get deeper into the crypt and find the crown. Once they reached the tomb and found the crown, a draugr said to be the remains of King Borgas rose. Once the draugr was defeated, Dranleic ran off with the crown to deliver it to Ulfric, as instructed by Galmar.

Whiterun city takeover

Ulfric's first target was Whiterun, because of its strategic location and because Balgruuf had yet to take a side in the civil war. As Galmar put it, "If he's not with us, he's against us." Before the attack, however, Dranleic was tasked by Ulfric with delivering an axe to Balgruuf. This is an ancient Nord tradition: if Balgruuf accepts the axe, it means he has decided to ally himself with the Stormcloaks; if not, it means war.

When Dranleic arrived in Whiterun, he walked steadily up to the jarl. Balgruuf, who had not seen Dranleic since he left for High Hrothgar, was pleased to see that he had made the trip. His mood quickly changed, however, as Dranleic presented the axe. Balgruuf returned the axe, and Dranleic was instructed to return to Windhelm. So he did. When he returned with the axe, Ulfric said, “I was wrong about him.” The Stormcloaks gathered at Whiterun's gates and rushed into battle. They fired fireballs from catapults, destroying some of the city's infrastructure and killing many men, with Dranleic killing the most. When they made it inside the city walls, they rushed to Dragonsreach to confront Balgruuf the Greater. There, Balgruuf and Irileth fought alongside each other against Galmar and Dranleic. They were no match for Dranleic, and Balgruuf surrendered. Turning to Dranleic, he said, “And you. A Stormcloak? I'd thought better of you. You'll all come to regret this day.”

Capturing the forts of Skyrim

Dranleic fought alongside other Stormcloak soldiers to capture the forts of several holds before the assault on Solitude: Fort Neugrad, Fort Sungard, Fort Snowhawk, and Fort Hraggstad. Taking Falkreath required the Stormcloaks to capture Fort Neugrad and rescue the Stormcloaks imprisoned in its dungeons. Dranleic stormed the fort, freed the prisoners alongside the other soldiers, and fought the Imperials from inside the fort. Fort Sungard in the Reach was swiftly taken by the Stormcloaks in the night, with Dranleic among them, and so was Fort Snowhawk, after Dranleic stole documents from an Imperial courier detailing plans to send reinforcements to the fort. The documents were then forged and delivered to General Tullius, and the fort was easily taken, since it never received the reinforcements it so desperately needed. Then all that was missing was to take Fort Hraggstad in Haafingar in order to weaken the empire and the defense of Solitude. Like the ones before the fort was swiftly taken by the Stormcloaks and Dranleic. They could now initiate their attack on Solitude and have Ulfric take his place as the rightful High King of Skyrim.

Battle for Solitude

See also: Battle of Solitude
Dranleic next to Galmar and Ulfric at the Castle Dour entrance during Ulfric's speech

On the [day] of [month], 4E 201, Ulfric and the Stormcloaks attacked Solitude, the capital of Skyrim. Dranleic was among the soldiers who fought the city's many Imperial defenders. Dranleic and the other soldiers made their way to Castle Dour to confront and kill General Tullius and Legate Rikke. Once inside the castle, few words were exchanged before both sides began to fight. As with Balgruuf and Irileth in Whiterun, neither Tullius nor Rikke was a match for Dranleic, and he was given the honor of beheading Tullius, as Tullius had nearly done to him at Helgen. Dranleic stood beside Ulfric and Galmar as Ulfric gave a victory speech at the castle entrance to the Stormcloak soldiers who made it through the battle. Much of the city's infrastructure was damaged, but it has mostly been repaired since. Most Imperials were slain in the attack on Solitude, and the survivors joined the Stormcloaks.

However, Ulfric could not yet become high king, because Elisif was still high queen and held the throne. He planned to marry her to secure his place as high king, but the arrangements have not gone through, and many wonder whether Skyrim will ever have a new high king before Elisif is no more. Still, the Stormcloaks' victory is very real: they have driven out most of the Imperials and the Empire, and citizens of Skyrim can rest more easily now that the civil war is more or less behind them.

Vampires

Few are aware that Dranleic has an alliance with the vampires in Skyrim. However, sources and rumors suggest that the vampires see him as a powerful individual and therefore do not engage him in battle. How exactly this came to be is unclear. Dranleic is not a vampire in any form, so it is unusual that the vampires do not attack or at least target him. Isran, the leader of the Dawnguard, explained that Dranleic betrayed the Dawnguard for his own gain by aligning himself with the vampires in order to have more authority in these already troubled times.

Dranleic walking together with Serana at Solitude

At that time, the Dawnguard was seeking new members as it reformed, and it had recently been attacked by vampires, as had the Vigilants of Stendarr. Isran reported that on the [day] of [month], Dranleic came to Fort Dawnguard wishing to join the Dawnguard and the Vigilants of Stendarr in ridding Skyrim of vampires and vampirism. He was given the task of finding out what the vampires were looking for, since Isran and the Vigilants theorized there had to be a motive behind the sudden attack. However, Isran stated he did not see Dranleic return after leaving Fort Dawnguard for Dimhollow Crypt alongside Tolan, a Vigilant of Stendarr who survived the vampires' attack.

Public perception

Dranleic is a rather controversial person in Skyrim. It is often debated whether he is or is not the figure depicted on Alduin's Wall or whether he has the blood of dragons at all. He is feared, doubted, and admired, all at once, by the citizens of Skyrim, and many still question his place in the province, where he comes from, and what authority he holds, such as within the Thieves Guild. The people of Skyrim can be split into three primary groups: the doubters, who believe Dranleic is a fraud and not a Dragonborn at all; the believers, who wholeheartedly believe Dranleic is a savior and a living legend; and the fence-sitters, who believe he is a Dragonborn, but not the one in the legend.

Doubters

Doubters are those who believe that Dranleic is neither a Dragonborn nor the figure from Alduin's Wall. There have been several pieces of evidence to suggest that he is at the very least a Dragonborn and might even be the fabled figure of the wall. Despite this, the doubters are stubborn in their belief that he is not the Dragonborn. The most vocal and active of doubters are the Dragon cultists, followers of the Dragon Cult led by the Dragon Priests, who worship the figure known as Miraak, whom they believe to be the one and only true Dragonborn, and who consider people like Dranleic to be frauds. These cultists once said directly to Dranleic's face that he is nothing but a fraud, and not a "true Dragonborn".

These doubters' beliefs are further strengthened by the acts of Dranleic, such as his killings and thefts. These people openly state their belief and share it with others, spreading a bad reputation for Dranleic. The biggest flaw in the doubters' logic is that Dranleic has slain Alduin and ended the Dragon Crisis for good, arguably making him the figure described in the legend. Even so, the doubters stick with the idea of Dranleic not actually being a savior or a hero.

Believers

Believers are the ones who wholeheartedly believe that Dranleic is a Dragonborn through and through, the savior of Skyrim and the figure from the legend of Alduin's Wall, for having slain Alduin. The believers are by far the biggest group of the three, praising Dranleic for his efforts and singing of his story and drinking to his name. When they see him, they know him. The believers know how strong he really is and what he's capable of. They have seen and heard Dranleic slay many dragons before, notably the dragon at Whiterun Watchtower. They've taken the words of the Greybeards as fact, and don't question their authority.

However, there are some holes in the believers' logic, as pointed out by some of the doubters. One that is often brought up is "how can we be sure he is the dragonborn when anyone could have killed Alduin. Dranleic just ended up taking all the credit." The doubters argue that Dranleic received help and only finished Alduin off. They say, "If he really is the Dragonborn, he should've been able to slay Alduin by himself no problem!" Others point out that people who aren't Dragonborn have learned to use the Voice. So, some doubters say, "With enough training, even Ulfric could've slain Alduin! If only he wasn't so busy with his damn Stormcloak Rebellion."

Fence-sitters

"Fence-sitters" is the nickname given to the minority of people who take no firm side between the doubters and the believers. They stand idly by and hear the opinions and mumblings of both the doubters and the believers, and take bits and pieces from their conversations and make up their own mind about the situation.

These people often come to the conclusion that Dranleic is Dragonborn, but might not necessarily be the one from Alduin's Wall. They believe he is Dragonborn because of the Greybeards' word, and the many eyewitness accounts of Dranleic absorbing the soul of a dragon – a trait which is unique to Dragonborn. They also hold that the legend of Alduin's Wall is only a legend, and need not be true. Some fence-sitters are also simply biased, possibly due to having been on the receiving end of Dranleic's tricks, or being against the idea of an Argonian Dragonborn, when most had expected a Nord or an Imperial.

Personal life

Dranleic is not open about his personal life, so most of what is known comes from accounts by his family and friends.

Dranleic lives happily with his spouse, Brelyna Maryon, and his steward, Jordis, at Heljarchen Hall, which lies on the border between the Pale and Whiterun hold. It is one of three properties where Dranleic owns land and has built a house. The other two are Lakeview Manor in Falkreath hold and Windstad Manor in Hjaalmarch.

Lakeview Manor is home to Lydia, Dranleic’s steward there. Brelyna describes it as his “storage home”, where he keeps his trophies and his arsenal of weapons and equipment for safekeeping. Dranleic makes regular visits to organize the house and store new items and loot, which Lydia says makes her happy, since it means she is not alone for long periods of time.

Brelyna says Windstad Manor is underused. It is where he does his research and practices alchemy and sorcery. She has never been to the house or seen what is inside, and her account is based on what Dranleic has told her.

According to Brelyna, Dranleic has no need for long adventures anymore after the Skyrim Civil War and the end of the Dragon Crisis. He has more or less settled down with her, and they are expecting a child soon. She says she is thankful they waited until all the chaos was over, as Dranleic was rarely home to spend time with her during the Dragon Crisis. She also noted that Dranleic supposedly has an idea for someone they should adopt as well, but he has not yet revealed to her who. She suspects he is still deciding himself. She thinks it is healthy for him to relax and stay home now, after all he has been through. She wonders if he will ever see his parents, but does not feel it is appropriate to ask.

Characteristics

Dranleic is a youthful, draconic Argonian with black-grey scales, ram-like horns, and a scar beside his left eye. He is best known for his heavy Daedric armor and for wielding two swords, and he is a feared warrior who also commands the power of the Voice. By most accounts he is quiet, focused, and short-tempered, makes his own decisions, and is loyal to few. He reveres the Hist and prays privately to Akatosh. He has also shown signs that suggest he sees Alduin as something of an idol. People close to him say there is a soft side beneath his silence.

Appearance

Dranleic is a youthful, manly, draconic Argonian with unweathered black-grey scales. His face is reminiscent of a dragon rather than an Argonian: he has a long snout, two curved, ram-like horns, a crest of red feathers for hair, small spikes along the jaw, a set of three spikes on his brows, and a long vertical scar beside his left eye. His eyes are small and reptilian, pale yellow-green with dark, vertical slit pupils. Like most Argonians, he has a scaly body and a long, jagged tail. His build is strong and agile, and he is vigorous in nature. He has defined muscles, claws, and sharp teeth, and he stands 189 cm (6 ft 2 in) tall.

Because of his black scales and dragon-like features, some people have begun to call him "Alduin’s Scale". Other names, such as "the Black Knight", "Rider of Night", and "Dragonborn of Darkness", are the most commonly used. These names stem from different aspects of Dranleic’s character, appearance, and nature.

Equipment

Dranleic owns several sets of armor, but the one he uses most, and is best known for, is his heavy Daedric armor. It is black, jagged, and spiked, with a red glow coming from under its plates. It has wide shoulder pads with big spikes that protrude and curve slightly, and similar spikes around the waist that curve upward and inward. Dranleic also has a helmet for this set, but he prefers not to wear it except in serious battles. The helmet is long to match his snout, and has tall spikes to represent his horns.

Dranleic also owns a Nightingale-style armor set, which he obtained during his adventures with the Thieves Guild. If he needs someone killed and needs to use the shadows to his advantage, this is the set he goes with. Some people theorize he wore it when murdering the emperor of Tamriel.

It is unclear why he owns a vampiric-style set, which includes robes, gloves, enchanted jewelry, and more. Dranleic is not known for being much of a wizard. However, it would seem he does practice sorcery and several schools of magic on his own. Some assume he got the set during his time close with the vampires.

Another set he used in the past is his smooth, black ebony armor, which he has kept as a memento rather than for practicality, as it was the armor he wore before his current Daedric armor. He also owns a set of Dragonscale armor, which is said to be made from the remains of several dragons he has slain.

He wields enchanted Daedric swords named Khalameet and Fortissax, which are serrated at the apex of their curves. He is also often seen with his curved ebony knife, Midir, and his large Daedric battleaxe, God’s Cleaver. It is uncertain what the names inscribed on Dranleic’s weapons mean, but researchers have suggested they are names of dragons of old.

Dranleic rides a large black horse that he named Manus, though it is generally referred to as Shadowmere. It has bright, glowing red eyes that pierce the darkness of the night, and it is outfitted with Daedric horse armor, handcrafted by Dranleic himself like all his other equipment.

Bandits tell stories of the warrior who rides in the night, warning of the bright red eyes that stare into the hearts of men, and have nicknamed him the “Rider of Night”. One such short story, “Beware the Rider of Night” by an unnamed bandit, describes Shadowmere as a horse of pure darkness, the essence of the abyss, yet living. The story claims the Dark Brotherhood gifted it to Dranleic while he was under their service, after a mission of revenge following a betrayal by one of their own. It cannot die, and he can call upon it at any time from anywhere, as it collects the shadows to manifest a bottomless black abyss from which it arises. Anyone who sees its red glowing eyes in a nightmare, the story warns, has “messed with the wrong people”.[ref]

Combat style

Dranleic is skilled in many forms of combat, including one-handed and two-handed weapons and dual wielding, but he prefers to dual wield swords. He fights relentlessly and without mercy, and is sometimes described as “blinding fast” and as strong as “Akatosh himself”. Dranleic is fearless and never backs away from opposition, should any dare offer it. His swordsmanship leaves his enemies with deep cuts all the way across their bodies, and he takes great pleasure in decapitating them. “Anyone daring to challenge Dranleic or place a bounty on him is sure to meet their end by his hand”, say the townsfolk of Whiterun.

Not only is Dranleic a feared swordsman, but he also wields the power of the Voice, or Thu’um. The Greybeards have said he is “truly gifted”. With the Thu’um, he can launch his enemies many feet away, incinerate them with the breath of dragons, mark them with death and weaken them to their very souls, command and bend the will of even dragons, and slow the fabric of time.

Personality

Dranleic is a quiet soul. He speaks only when necessary, keeps to himself otherwise, and exchanges few words in public. Those who know him say that beneath this silence, his heart burns with a fiery passion for power and revenge. He is always focused and serious, and approaches his own tasks in an orderly way, one step after another, wasting no time and getting things done. He is also short-tempered, and very aggressive toward those who oppose or speak lowly of him; making him angry only makes him more so. He is stubborn and never backs down from a challenge or a battle. For him, every fight is a matter of life or death.

Dranleic does not like to be told what to do. He makes his own decisions according to what benefits him most, and he is not easily deceived. It is said that when someone orders him to kill a person he does not want to kill, he kills the one giving the order instead. According to people close to him, he is not purely opportunistic, and he does not lack empathy or forgiveness. He has reportedly helped strangers and acquaintances alike without expecting anything in return, though he sometimes receives something for it, anything from a thank-you to a house. When Saadia was being hunted by a band of Alik’r, Dranleic went to speak with her and, though she was defensive at first, sided with her and killed the entire band at their hideout, asking for nothing but her thanks. On another occasion, he promised to recover the stolen necklace of an Argonian woman, who had little money, and did so, accepting only the spare change she offered.

He takes great pride in his craftsmanship and the things he makes. He built his three houses on his own, and he made all the equipment he uses himself.

He is not known to be loyal to anyone apart from his spouse, Brelyna Maryon, his steward, Lydia, and Ulfric Stormcloak. His spouse seems to have found a soft side in him, and the two are known to share a loving bond. He is said to be slightly biased toward his own kind, Argonians, though he would not hesitate to kill one if he had to.

Beliefs

Although Dranleic shares many similarities with dragons, he still identifies as an Argonian, which is why he reveres the Hist. The Hist are a species of giant spore trees growing in the innermost swamps of Black Marsh, which the Argonians worship because they believe the Hist created them, guide their physical development, and recycle their souls. Dranleic prays to the Hist once every day.

Besides the Hist, Dranleic actively prays to Akatosh, the father of dragons. He does so only in private, and the reason is unclear: neither Dranleic nor his acquaintances have explained it.

Dranleic has also shown some degree of worship toward Alduin, which people theorize is because of Dranleic’s similarities to Alduin and because Alduin is the entity most closely related to Akatosh. This is shown in a note written by Dranleic in Dovahzul:

"Oo, Alduin, fahvos irkbaan hi mii ful? Nis hi mindrus gein wo brud sos do hin bormah? Uv ru hin rahgot ativ un eylok vahzahgaar ful dil?" O, Alduin, why do you hate us so? Can't you recognize one who bears the blood of your father? Or does your anger towards our kind truly run so deep?
